## Planted problems in the raw files (Modules 02 and 03)

- orders_raw.csv has 16,094 rows but 15,904 distinct order_id values. 190 order_ids appear twice (exact duplicate rows from a re-import), worth $56,647.
- 45 test orders carry customer_id 0, order_id 900001 and up, and totals of $1.00 or $9,999.99 (worth $210,024). They have no order_items rows.
- 400 orders placed from January to March 2023 use US-style dates (MM/DD/YYYY) in both order_date and ship_date, a leftover from a system migration. 165 of them have a day number of 12 or less, so a value like 03/04/2023 can be read two ways. Only days 13 to 31 give the format away. The check: order_id values rise with time, so sort by order_id and confirm the dates rise with them.
- customers_raw.csv region has 24 distinct spellings for 6 real regions: "MW", "Mid-West", "Midwest", "N.E.", "NE", "NW", "Northeast", "Northeast ", "Northwest", "Pacific NW", "S.E.", "SE", "SW", "Southeast", "Southwest", "W", "West", "Western", "mid west", "north east", "north west", "south east", "south west", "west".

- products_raw.csv color has 30 spellings for 10 base colors (including red variants: Red, red, RED, Light Red, auburn, Crimson, Brick Red). ## Module 03: cleaning the raw files and tying out to finance

Cleaning recipe: drop exact duplicate order rows (keep one per order_id), drop test orders (customer_id 0), drop cancelled orders, convert the January to March 2023 dates from MM/DD/YYYY to year-month-day, then total order_total by the month of ship_date and subtract refund_amount by the month of return_date.
- Result: the recipe matches finance_monthly_revenue.csv in every one of the 36 months, with a largest difference of $0.00.
- Region labels: 24 spellings collapse to 6 (Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Southwest, West, Northwest). Missing age bands: five spellings collapse to one missing value. email_opt_in: eight spellings collapse to 1 or 0.
- If a learner skips the date fix, January to March 2023 will not tie out (the ship month is misread), which is the quickest way to prove the dates matter.
- Fan-out demonstration: joining orders_raw (with its 190 duplicate order rows) to order_items_raw counts those orders' lines twice, adding about $56,646.70 to revenue.

## Module 06: why did repeat purchasing slip?

Population: consumer customers whose first shipped order fell between January 1 and September 30 of the year, so every customer has a full 90-day window. Repeat means a second shipped order within 90 days after the first.

| Channel | 2024 customers | 2024 share | 2024 repeat rate | 2025 customers | 2025 share | 2025 repeat rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| organic_search | 730 | 49% | 45.6% | 598 | 32% | 46.8% |
| email_referral | 453 | 30% | 40.0% | 340 | 18% | 44.7% |
| paid_social | 319 | 21% | 20.1% | 915 | 49% | 23.1% |
| **All consumers** | 1502 | 100% | **38.5%** | 1853 | 100% | **34.7%** |

Every channel's repeat rate went up while the overall rate went down. The overall rate fell because paid social, the channel with the lowest repeat rate, grew from about a fifth of new customers to about half. That is Simpson's paradox at work, a mix shift. Wholesale customers (sales_rep channel) are excluded here and analyzed separately because there are too few of them for stable rates.
- Paid social spend: 2023 $29,865, 2024 $54,891, 2025 $135,702. Customers acquired through paid social: 266, 487, 1386. Spend per paid-social customer: $112, $113, $98.
- Correlation, monthly email_sends against monthly net revenue: r = 0.82. After removing each year's overall level (growth) and each calendar month's usual pattern (the season) from both series, r = -0.12. Email volume and revenue both climb with the holiday season and with company growth. Email may still help, but this correlation does not show it.

## Module 07: forecasting and the churn claim

Train on January 2023 to September 2025 net revenue, forecast October to December 2025, and compare with what happened:

| Method | Oct | Nov | Dec | Error (MAPE) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Actual | $183,257 | $250,222 | $299,207 | |
| Flat: average of the last three months | $174,844 | $174,844 | $174,844 | 25.4% |
| Seasonal naive: same month last year | $100,861 | $170,747 | $226,068 | 33.7% |
| Seasonal naive times year-to-date growth (x1.58) | $159,063 | $269,277 | $356,522 | 13.3% |

Each simple method fails in its own way. The flat forecast ignores the holiday peak. Same-month-last-year captures the season but ignores that the company grew by more than half. Combining season and growth roughly halves the error compared with the flat forecast, and even that method misses by double digits, which is why a forecast should carry a range and not a single number.

Vendor claim: "Our churn model is 95 percent accurate." The pilot file scores 2,000 customers. 100 actually churned (5.0%).
- Confusion matrix: true positives 30, false positives 22, false negatives 70, true negatives 1878.
- Accuracy: 95.4%. A model that predicts "no one leaves" scores 95.0%, so the vendor's 95% adds almost nothing over doing nothing.
- Recall (share of leavers caught): 30%. Precision (share of flagged customers who really leave): 58%. The model misses 70 of 100 leavers.

### Module 06 numbers (Activities 1 and 2)

| Year | Customers | Repeat rate | 95% margin of error | Range |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 2024 | 1,502 | 38.5% | plus or minus 2.5 points | 36.0% to 40.9% |
| 2025 | 1,853 | 34.7% | plus or minus 2.2 points | 32.5% to 36.9% |

The difference is 3.8 points, and the margin of error on the difference itself is plus or minus 3.3 points (1.96 times a standard error of 1.67). The two yearly ranges overlap a little, but the difference is still larger than its own margin, so a drop this size is unlikely to be noise alone. Overlapping ranges are a rough screen, and the difference's own margin is the better test.

Decomposition of the overall change (2024 to 2025): mix effect (the shift toward paid social) -6.5 points, rate effect (each channel's own change) +2.7 points, total -3.8 points. If 2025 had kept 2024's channel mix, the overall repeat rate would have been 41.1%, higher than 2024's 38.5%.

How to compute it: for each channel, multiply the change in its share of new customers by its 2024 repeat rate (mix effect), and its 2025 share by the change in its repeat rate (rate effect). The two effects add up to the total change.

Scenario assumptions (invented for the exercise, and learners should challenge them):

- Option A, expand paid social with $150,000: Low = cost per customer 30% higher, 365-day gross profit $130, half the customers incremental. Base = cost per customer $98, 365-day gross profit $157, 75% incremental. High = cost per customer $98, gross profit $175, all incremental.
- Option B, retention program costing $60,000: spend per active customer rises 2% (Low), 5% (Base), 8% (High).
- Option C, fit tools costing $30,000: fit-related returns fall 10% (Low), 25% (Base), 40% (High), recovering that share of the forgone gross profit.
- Probabilities: Low 25%, Base 50%, High 25%.

| Option | Low | Base | High | Expected value | Chance of losing money |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A: expand paid social ($150,000) | -$73,398 | $30,343 | $118,106 | $26,348 | 25% |
| B: retention program ($60,000) | -$43,198 | -$17,996 | $7,206 | -$17,996 | 75% |
| C: fit tools ($30,000) | -$28,016 | -$25,040 | -$22,064 | -$25,040 | 100% |
| D: hold the money in reserve | $0 | $0 | $0 | $0 | 0% |

Net values are twelve-month gross profit minus the spend. Option A has the only positive expected value and also the widest spread, and its payoff depends on a number the company hasn't measured (how many paid-social customers are truly new). That points toward a staged decision: a smaller, controlled test first.
